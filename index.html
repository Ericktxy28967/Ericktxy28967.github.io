Course Overview

GEC09 (Life of Rizal) covers the biography, works, and legacy of Dr. Jose Rizal — the national hero of the Philippines. This e-portfolio summarizes what I learned in the subject and how it affected my view as a student and citizen.

Key Learnings

  • Rizal's Early Life: I learned about his upbringing in Calamba, Laguna — how family, education, and early experiences shaped his character.
  • Education and Travels: Rizal's studies in Ateneo and in Europe expanded his worldview; his travels exposed him to liberal ideas and reforms.
  • Major Works: Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo are critiques of social injustice and the abuses of the Spanish colonial system and the friars.
  • Nationalism and Reform: Rizal advocated peaceful reform, education, and civic responsibility rather than violent revolution.
  • Sacrifice and Legacy: His martyrdom in 1896 made him a symbol of Filipino nationalism and inspired later movements for independence.

Personal Reflection

As a 3rd year BSIT student, one might think Rizal's life is distant from technology and code. But what stood out to me was his commitment to learning, discipline, and public service. Rizal's curiosity, critical thinking, and capacity to write persuasive arguments remind me that technical skills must be paired with ethics and civic awareness. Understanding history helps me appreciate the freedoms I enjoy and encourages me to use my skills for community benefit.

Article — What I Learned from GEC09: Life of Rizal

Life of Rizal was more than memorizing dates and events. The course invited me to read Rizal not as an untouchable hero in a textbook, but as a human being whose strengths and weaknesses shaped a nation's story. Through his novels, I saw how writing can expose injustice and move people toward reflection. The discipline that Rizal showed in self-study and his passion for reform inspired me to think about how I can apply the same dedication in my studies.

Rizal's belief in education as a tool for empowerment resonates with me as a student. I realized that learning technical skills—like programming or networking—is valuable only if I also learn how to communicate, analyze problems ethically, and use knowledge responsibly. Rizal's life taught me humility: he questioned authorities with evidence and reason, and he stood by his principles even when it cost him his life.

Finally, the story of Rizal reminded me of the power of small actions. Rizal's essays, letters, and novels were not loud revolutions, but carefully crafted ideas that spread and influenced others. As I grow as a future IT professional, I aim to follow his example by creating useful, ethical projects that contribute to my community.
